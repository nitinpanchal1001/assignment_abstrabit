# Meridian Health — Patient Intake and Triage Policy

Document CH-2291 · Version 4.1 · Approved by the Clinical Governance Board, 8 February 2026

## 1. Scope

This policy covers patient intake at all Meridian Health community clinics. It
applies to reception staff, triage nurses, and duty clinicians. It does not
cover emergency department intake, which follows the acute pathway.

The controlled reference for this policy is **CH-2291**. Any printed copy is
uncontrolled and must be checked against the intranet version before use.

## 2. The 72-hour callback window

Every patient assessed as **routine** at triage must receive a clinical callback
within **72 hours** of their initial contact.

The window is measured from the timestamp of first contact, not from the
completion of triage. Weekends and bank holidays are included in the 72 hours;
the window is not paused.

If a callback cannot be completed within the window, the case is escalated to
the duty clinician and recorded as a callback breach. Callback breaches are
reported monthly to the Clinical Governance Board.

## 3. Triage categories

| Category | Definition | Response target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate | Life or limb threatened | Same hour, escalate to acute pathway |
| Urgent | Significant deterioration likely within 24h | Clinical contact within 4 hours |
| Routine | Stable, no expected deterioration | Callback within 72 hours |
| Advice only | No clinical assessment required | Written response within 5 working days |

Triage category is set by a registered nurse. Reception staff must never assign
or change a triage category.

## 4. Re-triage

A patient must be re-triaged if any of the following occur:

- The patient or a carer reports new or worsening symptoms.
- More than 14 days have elapsed since the original triage without contact.
- A clinician requests re-triage for any reason.

Re-triage resets the response target. A routine patient re-triaged as urgent
moves to the 4-hour target immediately.

## 5. Interpreting and accessibility

Interpreting must be offered at first contact where a language need is recorded
or suspected. Family members must not be used as interpreters for clinical
content, other than to establish an immediate safety need.

Patients with a recorded communication need must have that need honoured at
every subsequent contact, not only at intake.

## 6. Consent and records

Verbal consent for triage is sufficient and must be recorded in the patient
record with the time and the name of the staff member who obtained it.

Records must be completed contemporaneously. A record completed more than 24
hours after the contact must be marked as a late entry with the reason.

## 7. Data handling

Patient-identifiable data must not be sent by unencrypted email or held on
personal devices. Where a clinician needs to work offsite, the approved remote
desktop is the only permitted route.

Breaches of this section are reportable under the incident response standard.
