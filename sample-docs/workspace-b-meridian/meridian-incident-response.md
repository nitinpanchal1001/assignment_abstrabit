# Meridian Health — Clinical Incident Response Standard

Document CH-3040 · Version 2.3 · Effective 1 April 2026

## 1. What counts as an incident

A clinical incident is any event that caused, or could have caused, harm to a
patient. Near misses are in scope: an event that was intercepted before reaching
a patient must still be reported.

Non-clinical events — estates faults, IT outages with no clinical impact, and HR
matters — are reported through separate routes.

## 2. Reporting timescales

| Severity | Definition | Report within |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Catastrophic | Death or permanent harm | 1 hour |
| Major | Harm requiring intervention or extended care | 4 hours |
| Moderate | Harm requiring observation or minor treatment | 24 hours |
| Minor / near miss | No harm reached the patient | 72 hours |

The clock starts when any member of staff becomes aware of the event, not when
it occurred.

## 3. Immediate actions

The first priority is always the patient's clinical safety. Staff must:

1. Make the patient safe and summon clinical help if needed.
2. Preserve any equipment, packaging or records involved, without cleaning or
   discarding them.
3. Inform the duty clinician.
4. Complete an incident record before the end of shift.

Staff must not alter an existing record to conceal or reframe an event. Where a
record is wrong, a correcting entry is added with its own timestamp.

## 4. Duty of candour

For any incident causing moderate harm or above, the patient or their
representative must be told what happened, in person where possible, within 10
working days. The conversation is documented, including who was present and what
was said.

An apology is not an admission of liability and must not be withheld on those
grounds.

## 5. Investigation

Moderate incidents are reviewed by the service lead. Major and catastrophic
incidents trigger a structured review led by someone independent of the service
involved.

Reviews focus on system factors. Individual performance is addressed separately
and only where a review identifies a specific capability or conduct concern.

## 6. Themes and learning

The Clinical Governance Board reviews incident themes quarterly. Any theme
appearing in three consecutive quarters is escalated to the Board with a
proposed intervention.

Learning from incidents is circulated to all clinical staff monthly. Circulation
is anonymised and must not identify the patient or the staff involved.
